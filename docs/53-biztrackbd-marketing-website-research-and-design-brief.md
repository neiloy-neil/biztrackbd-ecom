# BizTrackBD marketing website: research and design brief

**Research date:** 17 October 2026  
**Scope:** design, content, SEO, performance, loading states, analytics, Meta Pixel and Conversions API planning. No implementation code is included.  
**Primary market:** Bangladeshi online sellers, especially Facebook/Instagram-first merchants and growing direct-to-consumer brands.

## 1. Executive recommendation

BizTrackBD should not look like another purple SaaS landing page with a gradient headline, floating dashboard cards, an AI sparkle, and twelve identical feature tiles. That visual language is now the category default. It makes a young product feel interchangeable.

The recommended creative territory is **The Commerce Control Room for Bangladesh**.

The recognisable website signature should be a live, understandable order journey:

> A customer message or storefront order enters BizTrackBD, the bKash/Nagad payment is checked, COD risk is reviewed, a courier is booked, the parcel is tracked, and the payout is reconciled.

This journey should appear as a restrained interactive rail on the homepage, then become the navigation model for feature pages, product demonstrations, sales decks, short videos, and retargeting ads. It makes the product memorable by showing the work it removes.

The core positioning should be:

> **Every order. Every courier. Every taka. Under control.**  
> Ecommerce software for Bangladesh that brings your storefront, orders, bKash/Nagad checks, COD risk, local couriers, and profit into one operating desk.

This is more defensible than “build a store in minutes,” which Shopify and nearly every local builder already owns.

Pathao Commerce is the most important new strategic competitor. Its official launch material describes storefronts, inventory, payments, delivery, chat, analytics, and trust ratings in one platform. BizTrackBD cannot safely assume that “built for Bangladesh” is unique anymore.

### 2.2 Category design diagnosis

The category repeatedly uses:

- a large “all in one” headline;
- a white or purple/blue hero;
- a dashboard mock-up floating on the right;
- integration-logo strips;
- icon-based feature cards;
- an unsupported or weakly supported metric row;
- a pricing grid and FAQ.

These ingredients are usable, but their standard arrangement is forgettable. BizTrackBD's current live homepage is clear and credible at first glance, but its centered heading, violet button, browser mock-up, three-step row, feature list, templates, testimonials, comparison, pricing, and FAQ follow the same SaaS rhythm.

The website should retain the clarity and discard the sameness.

### 2.3 Proof is more important than polish

The repository's own brand work correctly identifies a critical proof gap. Current public merchant stories and numerical outcomes must not be presented as real unless the merchant, store, dates, measurement method, and written permission are available.

Before redesign launch:

1. Replace invented or unverified testimonials with labelled demo scenarios.
2. Do not publish “80% fewer returns,” “under one second,” annual savings, merchant counts, or similar numbers without dated evidence.
3. Maintain a claims register with source, owner, last checked date, allowed wording, and expiry/review date.
4. Use product proof while customer proof is being earned: live demo, sample order, real UI, security explanation, export demonstration, and documented integration status.

## 3. Audience and message hierarchy

### Primary audience: social-commerce seller becoming a real operation

They take orders through Facebook, Messenger, WhatsApp, phone, and a storefront. Their pain is copying addresses, verifying money, booking couriers, chasing customers, handling rejected parcels, and not knowing true profit.

**Hook:** “Your orders should not live in five tabs.”  
**Promise:** One operating desk from order to payout.  
**Proof:** Run a sample order without signing up.

### Secondary audience: growing D2C brand

They need staff roles, reliable stock/order workflows, branded storefronts, repeat purchase tools, reporting, and predictable local integrations.

**Hook:** “Growth should create more orders, not more operational chaos.”  
**Promise:** Repeatable workflows for the whole team.  
**Proof:** Feature depth, security, exports, audit trail, and case studies.

### Tertiary audience: Shopify/WooCommerce evaluator

They compare ecosystem, control, payments, plugins, maintenance, currency, and courier work.

**Hook:** “Choose for the market you sell in.”  
**Promise:** Less integration work for Bangladesh operations.  
**Proof:** A fair, dated capability and total-cost comparison.

## 4. Creative direction: “Boardroom meets bazaar operations”

The tone should be confident, practical, and locally literate—never folkloric, noisy, or “tech futuristic.” It should feel like serious business software that happens to understand New Market, Gausia, Chattogram, Sylhet, COD, and bKash.

### 4.1 Visual system

- **Base:** warm paper/off-white instead of clinical white; near-black “ledger ink” for authority.
- **Brand accent:** retain BizTrack violet but use it selectively for actions and active states, not as a full-page gradient.
- **Operational colours:** green for paid/delivered, amber for review/hold, red only for failure/risk, blue for courier movement. These colours always carry meaning.
- **Typography:** a characterful but highly readable grotesk for English paired with a first-class Bangla family. Use no more than two font families and two or three weights. Self-host WOFF2 subsets.
- **Photography:** real merchants, packing desks, shelves, phones, labels, delivery hand-offs, and local work environments. Avoid polished stock founders staring at laptops.
- **UI imagery:** actual product screens with legible focal points. Crop to the decision being demonstrated; do not show entire unreadable dashboards.
- **Illustration:** restrained line diagrams inspired by order slips, route labels, receipts, and ledger marks. No generic 3D blobs, glass spheres, chrome objects, or AI robots.
- **Grid:** editorial, asymmetric, and spacious. Alternate dense operational evidence with calm explanation.

### 4.2 Memorable brand devices

1. **The order rail:** one continuous line moving through Order → Verify → Protect → Ship → Track → Reconcile.
2. **Taka ledger marks:** small tabular-number treatments used for savings, fees, and profit explanations.
3. **Status stamps:** PACKED, PAYMENT CHECKED, RISK REVIEW, COURIER BOOKED—used as editorial labels, not decorative stickers.
4. **Bangla/English switch moments:** paired copy where it proves the product, rather than a token Bangla heading.
5. **Operational soundness:** subtle print/receipt transitions, never fake terminal animations.

## 5. Motion and interaction rules

Motion should explain state change. If an animation does not teach, reassure, or show cause and effect, remove it.

### Signature homepage interaction

The hero contains a working sample order card. A visitor can choose “COD” or “bKash,” then press **Run this order**. The rail advances through 4–6 states with short, truthful product explanations. The sequence ends at a mock profit/payout summary and offers **Try the full admin demo**.

This is not a video. It should be semantic content with progressively enhanced interaction so the message remains indexable and usable without JavaScript.

### Motion timing

- 120–180 ms: hover, focus, small state confirmation.
- 220–320 ms: drawer/card transition.
- 450–700 ms: one narrative step on the order rail.
- Never run multiple large animations at once.
- Stop autoplay after one cycle and provide user control.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`; the full story becomes a static sequence.
- No scroll-jacking, cursor replacement, endless marquee, autoplay audio, or decorative parallax on mobile.

### Micro-interactions worth using

- Courier selection updates expected workflow beside it.
- Pricing calculator updates the relevant plan, not just a number.
- Comparison filters change “best for” recommendations.
- Feature screenshots annotate one hotspot at a time.
- FAQ links update the URL fragment for sharing.
- Template previews switch desktop/mobile without layout jump.

## 6. Page-by-page website design

### 6.1 Global header and navigation

**Navigation:** Product, Solutions, Templates, Pricing, Resources, Company.  
**Persistent actions:** Log in, Try live demo, Start free.

Product opens a structured mega-menu by workflow: Sell, Manage, Deliver, Protect, Grow, Understand profit. Solutions is organised by merchant situation, not internal feature names.

On mobile, keep **Start free** visible but do not cover content with two sticky bars. The language control must say **বাংলা / EN**, not use a flag.

### 6.2 Homepage `/`

**Search intent:** ecommerce software Bangladesh; online store builder Bangladesh.  
**Job:** make the positioning understood in ten seconds and lead to a product experience.

Recommended sequence:

1. **Hero**  
   Eyebrow: `ECOMMERCE OPERATIONS FOR BANGLADESH`  
   H1: `Every order. Every courier. Every taka. Under control.`  
   Copy: `Build your store, manage orders, check bKash and Nagad, reduce risky COD shipments, book local couriers, and see what you actually earned—from one desk.`  
   CTAs: `Run a sample order` and `Start 14 days free`.  
   Proof line: `No card needed · Plans in taka · 0% sales commission` only if each remains accurate.
2. **Interactive order rail** with COD/bKash paths.
3. **The messy-before view:** a composed set of Messenger, spreadsheet, courier portal, wallet, and notebook fragments collapsing into one BizTrack order. Keep it visually controlled, not chaotic.
4. **Outcome navigation:** “Sell / Protect / Deliver / Reconcile / Grow.” Each is a deep link, not a generic card.
5. **Product chapters:** alternating full-width stories with real screen crops.
6. **Why local operations matter:** transparent explanation of direct wallet payment, local address structure, courier keys, Bangla SMS, and mobile operation.
7. **Template gallery:** three strong examples with live demos; link to the full library.
8. **Proof section:** verified merchants only. Until then, show “Pilot results coming” plus the live demo and engineering/security proof.
9. **Fair alternatives block:** BizTrackBD, Shopify, WooCommerce, and Pathao Commerce by fit—not a “we win every row” table.
10. **Pricing preview** with monthly/yearly toggle and plan recommender.
11. **FAQ** answering objections in plain language.
12. **Final CTA:** `Put your next order through BizTrackBD.`

### 6.3 Product overview `/product`

Use the order rail as the page spine. Each chapter explains inputs, action, result, and related integration. Include a sticky mini-map of the lifecycle. This page is for evaluators who need the whole model before selecting a feature.

Sections: Capture orders → Verify payment → Review risk → Manage stock/order → Book delivery → Handle returns → Reconcile COD → Retain customers → Measure profit.

### 6.4 Store builder `/features/store-builder`

**Intent:** online store builder Bangladesh.  
Lead with a side-by-side builder interaction, not “beautiful themes.” Show draft, mobile preview, sections, publish, history, and rollback. Explain what can and cannot be customised. Include store speed and SEO principles only after real measurement.

CTA: `Open the builder demo`.

### 6.5 Orders `/features/order-management`

Tell the story of one order from all channels. Demonstrate filters, quick view, staff actions, statuses, returns, exchanges, and inventory consequences. Add an “old way / BizTrack way” timeline with minutes and steps only when observed and sourced.

### 6.6 COD protection `/features/cod-fraud-protection`

This can become the strongest acquisition page and free-tool funnel.

Structure: cost of a rejected parcel → what BizTrackBD checks → what a flag means → recommended merchant action → privacy/fair-use explanation → free checker/demo → FAQ.

Never imply a person is a fraudster. Say “delivery history,” “risk signal,” and “review before shipping.” Explain false positives and the merchant's responsibility. This page needs legal/privacy review.

### 6.7 Couriers `/features/courier-automation`

Use a provider-neutral control panel showing Steadfast, Pathao, and RedX only where each integration is verified live. Show booking, label, status, return, and payout workflow. Individual integration subpages can target specific searches and contain setup guides.

### 6.8 Payments `/features/bkash-nagad-cod`

Clarify manual TrxID verification versus any automated gateway. Show exactly where money goes and what BizTrackBD does not hold. Separate merchant subscription payment from shopper payment to prevent confusion.

### 6.9 Profit and COD reconciliation `/features/profit-cod-reconciliation`

This is a high-value differentiator. Animate a sale price being reduced by product cost, courier fee, discount, return/refund, and ad spend until true profit remains. Use a ledger aesthetic and plain formulas. No invented uplift claim.

### 6.10 Marketing measurement `/features/meta-pixel-capi`

Design this as a trust page, not a tracking buzzword page. Explain browser Pixel + server CAPI, deduplication, standard events, consent, and what data is never sent. Show an event health panel and the difference between a browser-blocked event and deduplicated server coverage.

CTA: `See the tracking checklist`, not “Track everyone.”

### 6.11 Solutions hub `/solutions`

Use problem-based entrances:

- Selling mainly through Facebook/Instagram
- Launching a first branded online store
- Shipping 20+ parcels a day
- Losing money to refused COD parcels
- Moving from Shopify/WooCommerce
- Running online and physical sales (publish only when the product is verified)

Each solution page needs a distinct workflow, proof set, FAQ, and CTA. Do not create dozens of thin industry pages.

### 6.12 Industry pages `/industries/...`

Launch only four with genuinely different needs: fashion, beauty, electronics/accessories, and food/organic products. Use industry-specific screenshots, attributes, returns issues, merchandising, and templates. Each page should contain at least one original checklist or calculator.

### 6.13 Templates `/templates`

Make this a working gallery rather than a screenshot wall. Filters: industry, catalogue size, visual tone, and key need. Every template card offers live store, mobile preview, admin demo, and “use this template.” Keep bespoke vertical behaviour separate from visual design.

### 6.14 Pricing `/pricing`

Lead with “Pick by monthly orders and team size.” Use a two-question plan recommender, then the comparison table. Show BDT price, VAT/tax treatment, overage behaviour, support level, limits, trial terms, cancellation, export, and what happens when a plan expires.

Avoid dark patterns: no preselected annual plan disguised as monthly pricing, no hidden “contact us” for ordinary limits, and no false countdown.

### 6.15 Live demo `/demo`

This should be a flagship conversion asset. Let the visitor choose a business type and one mission: verify bKash, review a COD order, book a courier, or edit the store. Provide a guided checklist and a persistent “Exit demo / Start your store” action. Track task completion, not every click.

### 6.16 Customer stories `/customers`

Use a consistent evidence format: merchant context, previous workflow, starting baseline, adoption date, features used, measured result, method, quote, store link, and consent. Allow industry/order-volume filters. If evidence is not yet available, do not launch a fake stories archive.

### 6.17 Compare `/compare`

Create a neutral comparison hub and a small number of maintained pages: Shopify, WooCommerce, and Pathao Commerce. Every page states “last checked,” sources official vendor material, admits where the alternative is better, and explains switching cost and data ownership.

### 6.18 Migration `/migrate`

Use a checklist journey: export → review → import → domain → payments/couriers → tracking QA → launch. State what migrates automatically, what is assisted, what cannot migrate, expected downtime, rollback, and pricing. Offer a migration-readiness assessment rather than an ambiguous contact form.

### 6.19 Free tools `/tools/courier-fraud-checker` and calculators

The courier checker can attract links and qualified leads, but it needs strong privacy, rate-limit, and fair-use language. Add a refused-parcel cost calculator, Shopify/local-stack cost calculator, COD profit calculator, and courier-label generator only when each tool provides real standalone value.

### 6.20 Resource centre `/resources`

Organise by the merchant's job, not publish date:

- Start an online business
- Take safer COD orders
- Choose and manage couriers
- Use bKash/Nagad professionally
- Run Facebook ads and measure them
- Manage inventory and profit
- Grow repeat purchases

Every article should have an author/reviewer, reviewed date, screenshots, and a next-step tool or product workflow.

### 6.21 Trust pages

Publish `/security`, `/privacy`, `/terms`, `/status`, `/data-ownership`, and a clear `/about`. Security should explain tenant isolation, encrypted integration secrets, backups, access controls, incident contact, and exports in merchant language. Do not expose implementation details that create risk.

### 6.22 Contact and sales

Offer WhatsApp, phone, and form routes with expected response hours. Ask the minimum qualifying questions. Do not place phone numbers, emails, free-text business problems, or chat contents in analytics events.

### On-page rules

- One clear search intent and one descriptive H1 per page.
- Put the direct answer/value proposition in the first 100–150 words.
- Use descriptive internal links such as “COD fraud protection,” not repeated “learn more.”
- Give every screenshot useful alt text that describes the workflow; decorative marks get empty alt text.
- Self-referencing canonical on indexable pages.
- Only publish Bangla/English `hreflang` alternates when both pages are complete equivalents; include reciprocal links and `x-default` where appropriate.
- Keep signup steps, account, admin, demo sessions, preview/draft URLs, and success pages out of the index.
- Separate evergreen routes from campaign landing pages. Do not let UTM URLs become canonical variants.
- XML sitemaps contain only canonical, public, indexable URLs with accurate modification dates.

### Structured data

- Homepage: `Organization`, `WebSite`, and accurate `SoftwareApplication`/`WebApplication` data.
- Product/pricing: `SoftwareApplication` with visible BDT `Offer` details.
- Articles/guides: `Article` or `BlogPosting`, author, reviewer where relevant, and dates.
- Hierarchical pages: `BreadcrumbList`.
- FAQ markup only for visible FAQs and without promising a rich result.
- Reviews/ratings only when visible, first-party, eligible, and verifiable. Never mark up demo testimonials.

Google recommends complete, accurate JSON-LD and requires the app name and offer price for Software App eligibility; markup must match visible page content.

## 9. Analytics, Pixel, CAPI, and attribution design

### 9.1 Measurement model

The marketing website is a SaaS lead/signup journey, not a merchant's ecommerce store. Its conversion taxonomy should remain separate from storefront ecommerce events.

| Funnel stage | GA4/event-layer event | Meta event | Key parameters |
|---|---|---|---|
| Qualified page view | `page_view` | `PageView` | page type, language, campaign landing group |
| Feature/template viewed | `view_item` or a controlled content event | `ViewContent` | content id/type; no personal data |
| Live demo started | `demo_start` | `ViewContent` | demo mission, template |
| Demo task completed | `tutorial_complete` | custom event if needed | task, completion state |
| Pricing/plan selected | `select_item` | `ViewContent` | plan, billing period |
| Signup started | `sign_up_start` | `Lead` only if qualified by policy | source page, plan |
| Account signup completed | `sign_up` | `CompleteRegistration` | stable event id, plan; no raw sensitive data in event payload |
| Contact or migration request submitted | `generate_lead` | `Lead` | form type, not free-text contents |
| WhatsApp/call clicked | `contact_click` | optional custom | channel, page |
| First store published | server-side lifecycle event | optional qualified custom conversion | acquisition ids joined in CRM |
| First paid subscription | server-side revenue event | `Purchase` only here | transaction id, BDT value, plan |

Google lists `generate_lead`, `sign_up`, `tutorial_begin`, and `tutorial_complete` among recommended GA4 events. Use prescribed events where they fit and document custom events centrally.

### 9.2 Meta Pixel + Conversions API

- Send important conversions from both browser Pixel and server CAPI where appropriate.
- Generate one stable `event_id` per business event and send the same event name and ID through browser and server so Meta can deduplicate.
- Use the actual event timestamp and original page URL.
- Capture `fbclid`/`_fbc` and `_fbp` only according to consent and policy.
- Hash permitted customer identifiers server-side before transmission; never put email, phone, name, address, free-text messages, courier risk results, or authentication data in URLs or browser data layers.
- Do not fire `Lead` on a button click. Fire it after a successful, accepted lead submission.
- Do not fire `CompleteRegistration` until the account exists.
- Do not fire `Purchase` for a free trial or an unpaid proof upload.
- Verify using Meta Test Events and monitor event match quality and deduplication before paid campaigns.

Meta's developer documentation was rate-limited during this research, so the final implementation team must revalidate current parameter and consent requirements directly in Meta's official documentation and Events Manager before release.

### 9.3 Consent and privacy

Provide clear choices for necessary, analytics, and advertising storage. Google Consent Mode changes tag behaviour based on consent; it does not replace a consent banner or legal assessment. Default and update states must be implemented deliberately for relevant regions.

The experience should include:

- a compact first-layer banner with Accept, Reject non-essential, and Manage;
- equal visual weight for accept/reject where required;
- a persistent privacy-settings link;
- consent state propagated to Google and advertising tags;
- server events suppressed or limited consistently with the user's choice;
- a documented retention schedule and deletion process.

### 9.4 Attribution that survives the signup journey

Store first-touch and latest non-direct touch: landing URL, referrer, UTMs, click IDs, timestamp, and campaign creative ID. Carry them into the account and CRM/server record without exposing them in public URLs after capture. Connect acquisition to activation milestones: signup, store created, product added, courier connected, store published, first order, and paid conversion.

The primary dashboard should report:

- visit → demo start;
- demo start → completed task;
- visit → signup;
- signup → published store;
- published store → first order;
- trial → paid;
- CAC by campaign and creative;
- time to first value;
- activation and retention by acquisition source.

## 10. Loading states and performance budget

An interactive site must still feel immediate on a mid-range Android phone and constrained network.

### Loading experience

- Render the complete headline, copy, and primary actions in server HTML.
- The hero's static first order state appears immediately; interaction hydrates later.
- Use skeletons shaped like the final content for data-driven demos. Never use a blank white screen or full-page spinner.
- Reserve dimensions for screenshots, video, logos, testimonials, and embeds to prevent layout shift.
- Use optimistic feedback only for reversible UI; signup and form success require confirmed server response.
- For route changes, retain the header and show a short top progress indicator only when navigation is not immediate.
- Errors explain what happened, preserve entered data where safe, and offer retry/support.
- Empty states teach the next action and use real product language.

### Performance targets at the 75th percentile

- LCP ≤ 2.5 seconds.
- INP ≤ 200 ms.
- CLS ≤ 0.1.
- Initial marketing-page JavaScript target: as small as the framework permits; set and enforce a route budget before implementation.
- Hero image/video: no autoplay video as the LCP element. Prefer an optimised AVIF/WebP poster or product UI composed in HTML/CSS.
- Do not lazy-load the LCP asset. Make it discoverable in initial HTML and prioritise it appropriately.
- Lazy-load below-the-fold video, comparison tools, and heavy demo modules.
- Use responsive image sizes, modern formats, explicit dimensions, and a CDN close to Bangladesh users.
- Limit third-party tags; load advertising tags according to consent and after critical content.
- Measure field data with real-user monitoring and CrUX/PageSpeed where available; lab scores alone are not the release gate.

Google's current LCP guidance stresses early discovery of the LCP resource, avoiding lazy-loading it, reducing render delay, and using field data alongside lab tools.

## 11. Accessibility and trust requirements

- WCAG 2.2 AA target; keyboard access for every interactive demo.
- Visible focus, 44px practical touch targets, semantic headings and landmarks.
- Never encode payment/risk/status only by colour.
- Pause/stop for motion and video; captions and transcripts for demonstrations.
- Reduced-motion version of all sequences.
- English and Bangla typography tested on real devices; do not shrink Bangla to fit English layouts.
- Forms have visible labels, examples, inline validation, error summary, and confirmation.
- Pricing and comparison tables work as structured content on small screens, not clipped desktop tables.

## 12. Conversion system

Use four CTA types consistently:

1. **Experience:** Run a sample order / Open live demo.
2. **Commit:** Start free.
3. **Evaluate:** Compare plans / See how it works.
4. **Assist:** Talk on WhatsApp / Get migration help.

Do not put all four in every section. Each page gets one primary conversion and one lower-commitment alternative.

The highest-value experiment sequence is:

1. Current “launch a store” hero vs “every order under control.”
2. Static mock-up vs runnable sample order.
3. Start-free primary vs live-demo primary by traffic source.
4. Generic pricing vs two-question recommender.
5. Feature list vs order-lifecycle narrative.

Predefine hypothesis, primary metric, guardrail metrics, minimum runtime/sample rule, and decision owner before each test.

## 14. Design acceptance checklist

The design is ready for implementation only when:

- a visitor can explain BizTrackBD's difference after seeing only the hero;
- the homepage still communicates without animation or JavaScript;
- every prominent claim has a proof status;
- every page has one audience, intent, primary action, and measurement plan;
- the order-rail interaction is understandable by keyboard and under reduced motion;
- mobile layouts are designed, not inferred from desktop;
- Bangla examples are real and reviewed;
- loading, empty, error, success, and consent states are designed;
- Pixel/CAPI deduplication and event ownership are specified;
- no personal or sensitive text enters analytics;
- Core Web Vitals budgets are part of design sign-off;
- the website feels like one product system, not a collection of landing-page trends.

## 15. BizTrackBD Business: business, service, and portfolio websites

### 15.1 Product architecture

BizTrackBD should become an umbrella with two primary product families and one upgrade path:

| Product | Customer job | Primary outcome | Signature journey |
|---|---|---|---|
| **BizTrackBD Commerce** | Sell products and operate an online store | Orders, fulfilment, payment checks, lower COD loss, profit | Order → Verify → Protect → Ship → Reconcile |
| **BizTrackBD Business** | Establish trust and turn visitors into enquiries | Calls, WhatsApp conversations, quote requests, appointments, reservations, downloads | Discover → Understand → Trust → Enquire → Follow up |
| **BizTrackBD Hybrid** | Generate leads and sell products from one brand site | Leads and commerce without two disconnected websites | Visitor chooses enquiry or purchase path |

**Business Website, Service Website, and Portfolio are not three separate products.** They are starting paths inside BizTrackBD Business. The onboarding, templates, page library, analytics, and structured features adapt to the selected path.

This avoids a confusing catalogue of mini-products and makes the upgrade story natural:

> Start with a professional business website. Add bookings, a menu, structured content, or full ecommerce when the business needs it.

The public marketing site should use the following top-level language:

- **Sell products** → BizTrackBD Commerce
- **Win customers** → BizTrackBD Business
- **Show your work** → Portfolio path inside Business
- **Take service requests** → Services path inside Business

### 15.2 Why BizTrackBD Business can stand out

The Bangladesh market is split between:

1. agencies selling one-time custom websites;
2. generic global DIY builders;
3. cheap templates with weak content, SEO, accessibility, and lead handling.

Local providers commonly compete on delivery time, ownership, bKash/Nagad payment, and custom design. Global platforms such as [Wix](https://www.wix.com/business/website) and [Squarespace](https://www.squarespace.com/templates/browse/topic/all-templates/type/portfolio) compete on template breadth and mature builder ecosystems. BizTrackBD should not claim superior design flexibility to those platforms.

Its stronger position is:

> **A professional Bangladeshi business website that does something after it looks good.**

That means:

- Bangla and English content managed properly;
- custom domain on every paid plan;
- phone-first editing;
- built-in call, WhatsApp, map, download, quote, booking, and reservation actions;
- a structured lead inbox instead of enquiries disappearing across messages;
- local-business SEO foundations;
- privacy-conscious conversion analytics;
- the ability to add commerce later without rebuilding the brand website.

This is a materially different promise from an agency handover and from a blank-canvas global builder.

### 15.8 Restaurant, doctor, and public-figure pages

These are vertical paths within BizTrackBD Business, not decorative templates.

#### Restaurant `/business/restaurant-websites`

Journey: atmosphere and offer → menu → hours/location → call/WhatsApp/reservation request. Include structured menu content, canonical QR menu, availability, dietary labels, gallery, and Restaurant schema. A request must remain “awaiting confirmation” until the owner responds.

#### Doctor or clinic `/business/doctor-websites`

Journey: verified credentials → practice areas → chamber/schedule → appointment request. Never collect symptoms, diagnosis, prescriptions, or clinical files in the pilot. Do not generate claims such as “best doctor” or guaranteed treatment. Use verified Physician/MedicalBusiness facts only.

#### Public figure `/business/public-profile-websites`

Journey: identity and work → media/achievements → appearances → booking/enquiry. Provide structured press links, gallery, event/appearance entries, and a professional enquiry form.

### 15.12 Business analytics and tracking

Business dashboards should never show ecommerce revenue widgets to a portfolio or service tenant.

Measure:

- visitors and page views;
- traffic sources and campaigns;
- service/project/menu views;
- call, WhatsApp, map, email, social, and download clicks;
- lead, quote, appointment, and reservation submissions;
- request status and response time;
- visit-to-lead conversion;
- QR-origin menu sessions;
- CV/profile downloads.

Recommended events include `view_service`, `view_project`, `click_call`, `click_whatsapp`, `click_map`, `download_cv`, `generate_lead`, `request_quote`, `request_appointment`, and `request_reservation`. Keep event names centrally allow-listed. Never send message text, health information, phone numbers, email addresses, internal notes, or form contents to analytics.

Meta Pixel and CAPI are useful for Business-plan tenants running campaigns, but server events must preserve consent and deduplicate against browser events. The platform should provide a plain-language event-health screen instead of merely accepting a Pixel ID.

### 15.13 Design relationship to Commerce

The two product families should look related without becoming visually identical:

- Same logo, type system, layout grid, motion principles, trust language, buttons, and navigation shell.
- Commerce accent: operational violet with status colours and ledger/order imagery.
- Business accent: deep teal or cobalt paired with warm paper, using visitor/lead pathways.
- Commerce uses dense workflow UI and financial states.
- Business uses editorial site previews, proof, contact actions, and lead cards.
- Hybrid visibly combines both accents only where both capabilities are active.

Do not let selecting a visual template silently enable a behavioural module. A restaurant-looking template does not create reservations unless that capability is enabled; a portfolio look does not create CV downloads unless configured.
